01 The 48-hour window

Your guests are never happier than at the dock. That's also when you're busiest.

Wash-down. Fillets. Fuel. Tomorrow's wind. By the time you'd think to ask for a review, the moment has passed — and it doesn't come back.

  1. 2:47 PM

    The best day of their summer. They'd do anything you asked.

  2. Tuesday

    The photos are still on your phone. The review never got asked for.

  3. Next spring

    They Google "charter near me" and book whoever shows up first.

02 How it works

Here's what happens after every trip. Automatically.

One trip, start to finish. Every message comes from your name and a local number, in your voice. Guests reply, and their replies land on your phone.

T+0 · Trip ends

Tightline sees the trip close.

Your booking calendar marks it complete — or you text done. Either way, the clock starts. You get 15 minutes to say skip if a trip went sideways.

T+15 min · Photos land

Guests get the photos. All the guests.

Text the hero shots to your Tightline number between spots. Every guest gets a gallery with your name on it — not just the one who booked. The other three leave a number to see it, and now they're your guests too.

T+2 hours · The review ask

One message, while the day still glows.

Routed to Google, TripAdvisor, or FishingBooker — whichever profile needs it most this month. Every guest gets asked. Every guest also gets a direct line back to you, so a bad day becomes a phone call instead of a one-star.

T+3 days · The referral link

Word of mouth, with a receipt.

A personal link with a credit for them and a friend. When the friend books, you see exactly who sent them — no codes, no "who told you about us?"

When the season turns · The rebook nudge

They came in September. Ask them in April.

A few weeks before the bite turns on, past guests hear from you — with their old date, if it's open, and a one-tap hold. You set your seasons once.

Anytime · A cancellation fills itself

An open date isn't a lost day. It's a text.

When a trip drops off your calendar, the date goes to past guests within driving distance — a handful at a time, so you don't overbook. First YES holds it. You find out when it's filled.

Open dates

A cancellation isn't a lost day. It's a text.

When a trip drops off your calendar, Tightline offers the date to past guests nearby — six at a time, so you don't end up with two parties at the ramp. First YES holds it and gets the deposit link. You get one text: filled.

Weather day? Text open sat and it does the same thing for any date you name.

  • Prioritizes guests within driving distance
  • Batches of six, 20 minutes apart, until it fills
  • Optional last-minute price, or full rate

08 Questions

The things captains ask before they say yes.

Something else? Text (843) 555-0142. A person answers, usually within the hour, unless we're on the water.

My guests are older and not big texters. Will this work?

Most of your guests already text you to confirm the meet-up time and ask what to bring. That's the channel Tightline uses. Texts get opened; emails from a charter mostly don't.

Guests who booked with only an email get the same sequence by email. Anyone can reply STOP once and never hear from us again.

I don't use booking software. I run off a paper calendar and my phone.

That's a lot of operators. Two options: forward your confirmation emails (from FishingBooker, PayPal, Venmo, whatever) to your Tightline address, or text the trip in: sat 6am mike adams 555-0134 party of 4. Tightline builds the trip from that and asks if anything's missing.

Is texting my customers like this legal?

Yes, done properly, and we do it properly. Consent is captured at booking — we give you the exact checkbox language for your booking page or waiver. Every message carries a one-word opt-out. Nothing goes out before 8am or after 9pm in the guest's time zone. Messages send from a registered local number in your business's name.

We also don't gate or pay for reviews, which is what actually gets listings suspended.

Does this work for hunting outfitters with draws, tags, and multi-day camps?

Yes. Set your season windows and application deadlines once. Tightline nudges last year's hunters before draw deadlines and before opener, handles multi-day trips as a single event, and knows the difference between a booker and the four guys who came with him.

The booker is one person. What about the rest of the party?

The photo gallery is how we meet them. Everyone on the boat wants the photos, and they leave a number to see them. From then on they're your guests — they get the review ask, the referral link, and next season's nudge, with their own consent recorded.

Can I approve messages before they go out?

Yes. Review mode is on for the first two weeks by default: every message comes to your phone first and a thumbs-up sends it. Turn it off when you trust it, leave it on forever, or use it only for the rebooking nudges. Your call.

Will guests know it's automated?

Messages come from your name and a local number, in the voice you picked, with your sign-off. When a guest replies, it goes straight to your phone and you answer like you always would. Guests experience a captain who follows up on time. That's all it is.

What happens in the off-season?

After 30 days without a trip, Tightline offers to dock itself at $19 a month. Season nudges and referral links keep working, because the off-season is exactly when next season gets booked. Trip messaging comes back automatically the day you book one.

How do I cancel, and what happens to my guest list?

Text cancel. You get a spreadsheet of every guest, number, trip, and consent record within the hour, and billing stops. The list was always yours.
